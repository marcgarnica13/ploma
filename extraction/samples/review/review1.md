Reviewer #1
Questions
1. Relevance: Does the paper fit in the scope of the conference and in the applied ADS track as described above?
Yes, the paper describes a football analytics system based on process mining that could be valuable for the coaching teams of football clubs.
2. Summary: The review begins with a summary of the main ideas and contributions of the paper.
The paper proposes the use of process mining as a tool to extract insights about the tactics and strategies of football teams based on the analysis of event logs (not entirely clear what an event corresponds to since every data provider uses different conventions), their transformation into possession sequences and the application of process mining algorithms to extract Petri Nets and Heuristic Nets. The authors also use a grid-based clustering and visualization of events as a helpful means of communicating the main insights to the stakeholders. The authors perform an analysis of football matches in the English Premier League 2017/2018 season and present their findings.
3. Main strengths: List at least three main strengths of the paper.
- The proposed approach seems to be the first time that process mining is employed to study football tactics.
- The related work description is well-written and informative.
- Some of the empirical findings include interesting insights in relation to the tactics employed by English football clubs.
4. Main weaknesses List at least three main weaknesses of the paper.
- There is no sufficient motivation on why process mining is the right tool for this kind of problem. One might employ a directed graph analysis and arrive at similar conclusions.
- The paper lacks the appropriate formalism and clarity in the description of the proposed framework.
- The evaluation is limited. There is no way to ensure that the extracted findings are valid and that they are indeed interesting for the key stakeholders.

While the paper presents an interesting approach to the problem of studying football tactics, I feel that it is still in a relatively early stage in terms of method formalization and validation. I would like to commend the authors for their interesting work and would recommend they consider improvements along the following directions:
- The paper lacks a proper formalism in presenting the framework. While it is understandable that some data-specific differences exist, the authors should try to set up a common notation and framework to properly define the atomic events, the event sequences and the analysis using the employed methods.
- There is no justification of why process mining is necessary to achieve the stated goals. The event sequences and their mapping on the court grid could well be represented by directed attributed graphs and methods from the field of graph analysis/mining could be employed to extract similar insights.
- Most importantly, the presented evaluation is quite limited. While the presented results seem interesting and intuitive, the authors should demonstrate that a) these results are indeed valid, i.e. an expert should corroborate that they are accurate, and b) the extracted insights should be useful to experts and add some more knowledge compared to what they already know about a team. Therefore, the evaluation should at least include some focus group discussion involving football experts (coaches, etc.) to validate results and assess their value.
- The visualizations of Figure 4 make it very hard to compare between different teams. I would suggest exploring ways to further simplify visualizations especially in comparison mode, e.g. show only paths/edges that are very different between the two teams, so that their unique features are highlighted.
- The writing of the paper could also be improved.
